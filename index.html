Getting started

Filling the shelf fast

Entering fifty bottles by hand is miserable. Let an AI do the typing.

  1. Photograph your bottles in groups of four to six, labels facing the camera, in decent light. One shot of a whole shelf from across the room gets you guesses.
  2. Tap Copy the AI prompt in the Backup menu. Paste it into any AI chat, attach your photos, and add anything the label does not say, like what you paid.
  3. Check the CSV it gives you. Spot check a few rows. Clone houses look alike and get misidentified, so the printed label wins over the AI every time.
  4. Import a spreadsheet from the Backup menu. Bottles are matched on name and house, so importing a corrected file updates rather than duplicates.
  5. Add photos yourself. Tap a bottle, Edit, tap the photo box. No AI can put pictures into this app, and the shot you already took is better anyway, it shows the real bottle at its real level.

What the AI gets wrong

Retail prices drift and every model has a knowledge cutoff, so treat them as rough. Obscure clones often have no published notes, in which case use the original's accords, since that is what the clone is chasing. The prompt tells the AI to leave a cell blank rather than guess, and blank beats wrong here.

Formats, and what the strengths mean

The format field is about how much perfume oil is in the bottle. More oil lasts longer and costs more. It does not mean it smells better.

  • Extrait or parfum, the strongest, six to twelve hours, one or two sprays.
  • Eau de parfum, five to eight hours, the default for most modern releases.
  • Eau de toilette, three to five hours, brighter opening, fades sooner.
  • Eau de cologne, one to two hours, the old citrus splash style.
  • Oil and solid, no alcohol, quiet but long lasting on skin, and they go on first when you layer.
  • Body spray and hair mist, light and short lived, supporting players.

If the box does not say, pick eau de parfum. And do not read strength as quality, a well made EDT can outlast a weak EDP.

Notes versus comments

Notes and accords should be scent words only, like pineapple, bergamot, birch, smoky woods. That field drives the layering suggestions. Put your own opinions in My comments instead.

Layering

Open a bottle and scroll down for its best partners, or open Layering from the menu for the whole shelf plus your saved blends. Scores come from shared accords, whether one lifts and one anchors, and season overlap. It is a starting point, not a verdict. Rate what you actually wear.

Backups matter here

Your shelf lives in this browser, not on a server. It does not follow you to another phone or browser on its own. Send a backup to Drive regularly, and always before switching devices or clearing your browser. Restore replaces everything, so use it deliberately. Undo the last change rolls back the last three.

New bottle

It is all about how much perfume oil is in the bottle. More oil means it lasts longer and costs more. It does not mean it smells better.

Extrait, also called parfum. The strongest, roughly 20 to 40 percent oil. Six to twelve hours. Sits close to the skin at first, then blooms. One or two sprays is plenty.

Eau de parfum, EDP. Around 15 to 20 percent. Five to eight hours. The default for most modern releases and the safest guess if the box does not say.

Eau de toilette, EDT. Around 5 to 15 percent. Three to five hours. Brighter and sharper on the opening, fades sooner. Common for fresh and citrus scents.

Eau de cologne. Around 2 to 5 percent. One to two hours. The old splash style, usually citrus and herbs. Not the same thing as calling a fragrance a cologne in everyday speech.

Oil. No alcohol, so it stays on the skin instead of projecting. Long lasting up close, quiet from a distance. Goes on first when you layer.

Solid. A balm in a tin. Very close to the skin, easy to travel with, easy to reapply.

Body spray. Roughly 1 to 3 percent. An hour or two. Cheap, meant for after a shower or the gym bag rather than as your main scent.

Hair mist. Low alcohol so it will not dry your hair out. Light and soft.

Two things worth knowing. Strength is not quality, a well made EDT can outlast a weak EDP. And houses label loosely, so if the box does not say, EDP is the sensible default.
